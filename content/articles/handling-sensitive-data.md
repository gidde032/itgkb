## Summary

Most data mishandling isn't malicious — it's someone emailing a spreadsheet that turned out to be
sensitive, or dropping it in an over-shared folder. The fix is a habit: identify how sensitive
something is before you store or share it, and default to the least exposure that still gets the
job done.

## Guidance

1. **Know the level.** Organizations classify data (roughly public → internal → confidential →
   restricted/regulated). Regulated categories (personal, health, financial, student records) carry
   legal handling requirements — when in doubt, treat it as more sensitive, not less.
2. **Store it in an approved place.** Sensitive data belongs in sanctioned systems, not personal
   drives, personal email, or a laptop's desktop.
3. **Share by least privilege.** Grant the minimum access to the minimum people; avoid
   "anyone with the link" for anything non-public (see sharing links & permissions).
4. **Minimize and dispose.** Don't keep sensitive data you don't need; delete/return it when the
   task is done.

## Resolution Steps

1. If sensitive data was exposed (mis-shared, emailed, lost device), report it through the
   organization's data-incident path promptly — disclosure timelines can be legally required.

## Notes / Edge Cases

- Aggregation risk: individually harmless fields can become sensitive combined (name + ID + date
  of birth) — classify the combination, not just each field.
- Screenshots, exports, and "just a copy for the deck" quietly spread sensitive data past its
  controlled home — treat copies as the real exposure.
