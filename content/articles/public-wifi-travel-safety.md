## Summary

Public wifi is mostly fine for browsing today because sites are encrypted (HTTPS), but it still
carries real risks: fake "evil twin" networks, captive portals that phish, and shoulder-surfing.
A few habits remove most of the danger without paranoia.

## Guidance

1. **Prefer your own connection for anything sensitive** — a phone hotspot or the organization's
   VPN beats an unknown network for logins and internal resources.
2. **Trust HTTPS, distrust certificate warnings.** A browser warning about a site's certificate on
   public wifi is a stop sign, not a click-through.
3. **Watch for lookalike networks.** "Airport-Free-Wifi-2" next to the real one is a classic trap;
   confirm the official network name, and be wary of captive portals asking for account logins.
4. **Reduce exposure:** turn off auto-join for open networks, disable file sharing, and keep the
   device patched.

## Resolution Steps

1. For a user who logged into something sensitive on sketchy wifi and is now worried, treat it like
   possible credential exposure: change the password and watch for unexpected sign-ins.

## Notes / Edge Cases

- The bigger travel risk is often physical — an unlocked, unattended laptop — not the wifi itself.
- Public charging ports are low-risk with a normal cable, but a charge-only cable or your own
  adapter removes even that concern.
